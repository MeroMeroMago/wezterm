## How it works on iOS vs Android; generic Android phones (Unihertz Titan, Clicks Communicator)

### Takeaway
You choose apps and websites in the Bloom app and tap the card to the back of the phone to start or end a "focus session". It works on both iOS and Android. On iOS it uses Apple's Screen Time (Family Controls) framework. That framework is inferred: the App Store listing and reviews mention Apple's app picker, and deleting the app removes the blocks. On Android it relies on the **Accessibility Service API**, which is inherently weaker because the user can revoke it in Settings. Android reliability has been mixed. Early Pixel reviewers found it "far too easy to bypass", while later reviews report improvement.

### Cited Findings
- **How it works:** "select the apps or websites you find most distracting, tap your Bloom card to your phone to block those apps, stay productive with your card out of reach, and when ready, just tap your Bloom card to your phone to unblock your apps." The card is a passive NFC chip with no battery. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bloom-conquer-screen-time/id6736728158); [bloom.inc](https://bloom.inc/)
- **Android mechanism:** "With your permission, Bloom can use the Accessibility Service API to prevent you from accessing different websites of your choosing." — [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.findyourbloom&hl=en_US)
- **General caveat on Android blockers** (not Bloom-specific): blocking "works through an Accessibility Service that you grant in Settings and can revoke in Settings, so the final switch always belongs to whoever is holding the phone." — [Habit Doom blog](https://habitdoom.com/blog/android-app-blocker-that-cant-be-bypassed) (a competitor blog; the point is generally accurate)
- **iOS evidence:**
  - App Store reviews complain that "Apple's app picker has selection limits and crashes frequently when setting up" — this is the Family Activity Picker from Apple's Screen Time API — [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bloom-conquer-screen-time/id6736728158?see-all=reviews) (via search summary)
  - "By default, deleting Bloom will unblock all of your apps" — [bloom.inc FAQ](https://bloom.inc/pages/faq) / [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.findyourbloom)
- **Android reliability:**
  - A Play reviewer said it "worked on their iPhone but doesn't work on their Samsung S26 Ultra", and that iPhone settings were easier to update. After reinstalling and recreating schedules it worked the next day.
  - Pixel reviewers found it "far too easy to bypass the lockout, at least on Pixel devices". The developer later responded that the issues were resolved.
  - A December 2025 edit to a review said the app "works much more smoothly and consistently".
  - Source for all three: [Google Play listing reviews](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.findyourbloom&hl=en_US) (via search summary)
- **Clicks Communicator:** an Android "companion phone" with a physical keyboard, unveiled at CES January 2026 and slated for Q4 2026 release. Per Wikipedia it may not be shipping yet as of late Sept 2026. — [Wikipedia: Clicks Communicator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clicks_Communicator)
- A search summarizer asserted that "Bloom is compatible with any Android phone that has NFC capability". **This is the summarizer's own inference, not a quoted Bloom statement.** — (search summary citing [bloom.inc product page](https://bloom.inc/products/bloom-card))

### Inferences
- On a generic Android phone such as a Unihertz Titan (which has NFC on most models) or a Clicks Communicator, Bloom should *in principle* work if three things hold: the phone has NFC, it has Google Play or can sideload the APK, and it allows Accessibility Services. Enforcement strength will depend on how aggressively the OEM kills background apps; Unihertz uses a MediaTek chipset with aggressive battery management. Expect the same weaknesses as other Android Accessibility-based blockers: the service can be revoked, and a force-stop may kill it unless Strict Mode covers that.
- The Clicks Communicator's NFC status and release status should be checked separately.
- Android 16/17 "Advanced Protection" restrictions on Accessibility services have affected other blockers such as AppBlock ([AppBlock notice](https://appblock.app/androids-new-advanced-protection-affects-appblock-from-blocking/)). Users with Advanced Protection enabled could likely see Bloom affected too. I did not verify this for Bloom.
- iOS enforcement is structurally stronger. Screen Time shields persist even when the app is force-quit, and the main iOS escape routes are deleting the app or revoking Screen Time access in Settings. Strict Mode targets the first of these.

### Gaps
- No Bloom-specific confirmation for Unihertz, Clicks or other niche Android devices. There is also no official list of supported Android versions.
- No snippet listed the exact Android permissions Bloom requests (Accessibility, Usage Access, Device Admin, Display over other apps).
- Whether Bloom works without Google Play Services (for example on degoogled ROMs).

## Features: breaks, scheduling, Strict Mode, emergency unlocks, lost card, Child Mode, own NFC tag

### Takeaway
Features include:
- **Breaks:** up to 3 five-minute breaks per session, configurable, no card needed.
- **Schedules** that start and end automatically.
- **Emergency exits:** 3 to start, plus 1 more each month; each unblocks without the card.
- **Strict Mode:** blocks deleting apps during a session and, per multiple sources, force-killing Bloom.
- **Child Mode:** disables emergency exits, prevents deletion and locks schedules; only the parent's card changes settings.
- **Website blocking** and adult-content blocking; stats, streaks and friend comparisons.

Details of Strict Mode's platform rollout conflict between sources. Using your own NFC tag was **not confirmed**.

### Cited Findings
- **Breaks:** "setting limits on the frequency and duration of breaks". Bloom allows up to three five-minute breaks per focus session; these unlock blocked apps without the card and re-lock automatically when time expires. Fortune: users "can choose to add one, two, or three five-minute breaks". — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bloom-conquer-screen-time/id6736728158); [whatifididnt Bloom vs Brick](https://whatifididnt.com/blog/bloom-vs-brick/); [Wholistic Productivity](https://wholisticproductivity.com/brick-vs-bloom-which-physical-app-blocker-is-worth-your-money/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/13/analog-gen-z-phone-addiction-bloom-brick-app-blockers-dumb-phones-social-media/)
- **Schedules:** "schedules for your focus periods allowing them to start and end automatically" — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bloom-conquer-screen-time/id6736728158)
- **Emergency exits and lost card:**
  - Exits "unblock all apps without your bloom card up to three times" — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bloom-conquer-screen-time/id6736728158)
  - FAQ: "three emergency exits if you lose your card", plus "an additional one every month" — [bloom.inc FAQ](https://bloom.inc/pages/faq)
- **Strict Mode:**
  - Official wording: "toggle 'strict mode' in settings, which prevents all apps from being deleted while in a focus session" — [bloom.inc](https://bloom.inc/) / [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.findyourbloom)
  - The App Store listing calls it "an optional Strict Mode that disables your ability to delete the Bloom app while in focus mode" — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bloom-conquer-screen-time/id6736728158)
  - Force-kill: "Bloom recently released an update introducing Strict Mode, which prevents users from force killing the Bloom app." Search summaries attribute this to the Google Play listing and whatifididnt. — [Google Play (en_CA)](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.findyourbloom&hl=en_CA); [whatifididnt review](https://whatifididnt.com/blog/bloom-card/)
  - Timing: "Bloom added a strict mode in 2026 that prevents force-closing the app to escape a block, after early reviewers found that gap." — search summary attributed to [BGR](https://www.bgr.com/2235201/phone-blocking-app-brick-alternatives/) / [autonomous.ai](https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/brick-vs-bloom) (exact source unclear)
  - The earlier bypass: users could "circumvent the app in three quick touches by dismissing recent apps" (the recent-apps swipe, typical of Android) — search summary of [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.findyourbloom&hl=en_CA) / reviews
  - **Conflict on platform:** one snippet says "Strict Mode is currently available on iOS, and Bloom has stated that Android support is in development" (attributed to the [whatifididnt review](https://whatifididnt.com/blog/bloom-card/)). Another summary says Play Store content indicates the force-kill Strict Mode "has been rolled out to the Android version as well" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.findyourbloom&hl=en_CA)). The whatifididnt snippet also says Strict Mode fixes the uninstall bypass "previously exploitable on Android devices". **Unresolved. The likeliest reading is that the uninstall-prevention Strict Mode shipped on iOS first, and the force-kill protection is on Android per the Play listing. Verify on a device.**
- **Child Mode:** "disables emergency exits, prevents app deletion, and locks schedules… Only a parent's card can change the settings, making Bloom like a car key for your kid's phone" — [bloom.inc FAQ](https://bloom.inc/pages/faq); [bloom.inc PDP](https://bloom.inc/pages/pdp)
- **Other features:**
  - Website blocking and adult-content blocking; the app can also block in-app purchases in other apps — [dumbwireless](https://dumbwireless.com/products/bloom-smartphone-blocking-card); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bloom-conquer-screen-time/id6736728158) (via summary)
  - Streaks, stats and friend comparisons ("gamifies staying off your phone") — [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bloom-conquer-screen-time/id6736728158?see-all=reviews) (via summary)
- **Own NFC tag:** a targeted search found **nothing** stating whether a generic NFC sticker or card can replace the Bloom card — [search results incl. bloom.inc product page](https://bloom.inc/products/bloom-card)

### Inferences
- Breaks and emergency exits make Bloom deliberately "softer" than Brick. They are good for moderate users and a loophole for people who compulsively use every allowed break.
- The monthly replenishing exit means a determined user always has at least one card-free unlock per month, unless Child Mode is on.
- Bloom calls itself a "zero-bypass tool", but that is marketing. Its own design includes card-free exits, and on iOS the Screen Time permission can still be revoked in Settings unless a Screen Time passcode is held by someone else. That last point is inference from how Apple's framework works; I did not see it tested for Bloom.
- Bloom probably pairs the app to its own cards via a UID or signed payload. If so, a generic NFC tag would not work, but this is unverified.

### Gaps
- Whether generic NFC tags work, and how cards are paired or registered to an account.
- Whether a new or replacement card can be paired after a loss without using an exit.
- Whether Strict Mode also blocks revoking Screen Time permission (iOS) or disabling Accessibility (Android).
- The exact release date of the 2026 Strict Mode update.
- Whether breaks are fully configurable (for example 0 breaks, or lengths other than 5 minutes). Sources say "limits on the frequency and duration".

## Brick vs Bloom comparison (with bias flags)

### Takeaway
Bloom costs **$39 for a steel card**; Brick costs **$59 for a magnetic cube**. Both are passive NFC with no subscription, and both run on iOS and Android. Bloom is **cheaper, softer and more family-oriented**: configurable 5-minute breaks, monthly-replenishing emergency exits and a Child Mode. Brick is **stricter** (no breaks) and has a longer track record and reportedly smoother UX. Several comparison articles come from competitors, so treat their verdicts with caution.

### Cited Findings
- **Common ground:** both are passive NFC devices with no battery or charging; both work on iOS and Android; both are free to use after the hardware purchase — [autonomous.ai](https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/brick-vs-bloom); [Wholistic Productivity](https://wholisticproductivity.com/brick-vs-bloom-which-physical-app-blocker-is-worth-your-money/)
- **Price:** "Bloom costs about $39 and Brick costs $59" — [autonomous.ai](https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/brick-vs-bloom); Brick at about $59 is also in [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/13/analog-gen-z-phone-addiction-bloom-brick-app-blockers-dumb-phones-social-media/)
- **Strictness:** "Unlike Brick, which is a total block, Bloom offers breaks." With Brick, "When your phone is Bricked, it stays locked until you scan the cube." — [autonomous.ai](https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/brick-vs-bloom); [Wholistic Productivity](https://wholisticproductivity.com/brick-vs-bloom-which-physical-app-blocker-is-worth-your-money/)
- **Families:** "Bloom is better suited to families because it includes a child mode… Brick does not offer a dedicated child mode." — [autonomous.ai](https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/brick-vs-bloom)
- **UX:** "Brick feels smoother and more seamless… one of the few practical areas where Brick clearly feels more polished." — [autonomous.ai](https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/brick-vs-bloom)
- **Verdict:** "Brick is worth it if you specifically want maximum strictness and a long track record, and you will exploit any break feature Bloom offers. If you have reasonable self-control or want a child mode and a nicer card, Bloom does more for less." — [autonomous.ai](https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/brick-vs-bloom) and/or [whatifididnt Bloom vs Brick](https://whatifididnt.com/blog/bloom-vs-brick/) (search summary blended both)

### Inferences
- For a buyer choosing between them:
  - **Bloom** fits if cost, a sleek wallet-sized card, breaks or kid control matter.
  - **Brick** fits if you know you will abuse breaks and exits and want no-escape blocking, and you value polish and track record.
  - Brick's own emergency-unbrick limits and Android strength should be compared from the Brick research notes.
- Bloom's card fits in a wallet. That makes it easy to carry, but it also defeats the "leave the key somewhere else" model unless the user deliberately stores it away.
