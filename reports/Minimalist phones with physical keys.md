# Buy the keyboard phone, then lock it down

The best setup for a US user who wants a physical keyboard, less screen time and working tap-to-pay, banking, 2FA, maps, rideshare and messaging is a **Google-certified Android keyboard phone set up to hold only your essential apps, with a physical-token blocker guarding everything else**. Keep your current smartphone at home for media. The strongest device for that role is the **Clicks Communicator**, which costs **$499 only through September 30, 2026 before rising to $649 on October 1** and doesn't ship until **December 2026** ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2262962/clicks-communicator-shipments-begin-in-december/)). The **Unihertz Titan 2 Elite** ($489.99, shipping now, NFC with Google Pay) is a close, proven alternative with the same 4.03-inch screen ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/unihertz-titan-2-elite-business-phone-review)). This conclusion comes from one hard constraint. Tap-to-pay by phone in the US needs Google Wallet on a Play-certified device, and that rules out every true dumbphone, the E-ink phones without Google services, de-Googled Android, and all the hackable Pi handhelds. The "hackbetty" (HackberryPi CM5) isn't a phone at all. The device alone won't cut your screen time, though. The best trial evidence says the benefit comes from removing mobile internet, not from adding keys. So the lock matters more than the phone, and **you can test the whole idea this week for $0–$59** using the Clicks Power Keyboard you already own plus a Brick or Bloom token. One caveat applies throughout. The researchers' full-page fetches were blocked, so nearly every fact here comes from search-engine snippets of the cited pages. Prices, ship dates and Wallet support should be treated as snippet-verified, not page-verified.

## Tap-to-pay alone eliminates nine of every ten "dumb" options

Every path the user asked about was checked against the six essentials. Most minimalist devices fail on the same two items: phone-based contactless payment and app-based banking. Google Wallet tap-to-pay needs a device that passes Google's Play Integrity checks ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/error-google-wallet-3213122/)). That's why the **Light Phone III ships with an NFC chip that still does nothing**. Light calls it groundwork for "future tools like a digital wallet" and has given no date ([Light blog](https://www.thelightphone.com/blog/light-iii-design-manifesto)). It's also why the de-Googled **Mudita Kompakt** has NFC hardware but no working Google Pay ([Mudita Forum](https://forum.mudita.com/t/nfc-usage-on-the-kompakt/7989)). GrapheneOS keeps most banking apps through sandboxed Play, but it **cannot produce the attestation Google Wallet requires** ([franklinetech](https://franklinetech.com/what-breaks-on-grapheneos/)). The Punkt MC03 has no Google Pay, and its Fidesmo payment integration is still in progress ([Punkt FAQ](https://support.punkt.ch/en-US/articles/aphyos-apps-422562)).

Keypad phones fare worse. WhatsApp on KaiOS is **completely dead**: new logins were blocked in June 2024, service ended around February 2025, and the app was pulled from the KaiOS Store ([FoneArena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/429156/whatsapp-kaios-support-discontinued.html)). That strands the Nokia/HMD 2780, TCL Flip and Kyocera Cadence class with calls, SMS and hotspot only. The locked-down Android flips (Sunbeam F1 Horizon/Pro) add hotspot and Waze on some models, but they **cannot send or receive RCS**. That means you silently miss messages in iPhone/Android RCS group chats unless friends fall back to MMS ([PauseGadget](https://pausegadget.com/sunbeam-f1-review/)).

The hackable devices are the clearest mismatch. The **HackberryPi CM5** is a Raspberry Pi CM5 Linux handheld with a reused BlackBerry keyboard and a 4-inch 720×720 screen. Its own documentation describes a "portable linux-powered computer" with **no cellular radio, calls or SMS** ([GitHub](https://github.com/ZitaoTech/HackberryPiCM5)). The Beepy has no cellular radio either, and its community lists it as inactive ([BBKB Community](https://bbkb-community.github.io/inactive-projects/beepy/)). The ClockworkPi uConsole's 4G add-on places calls through raw `ATD` commands and a wired headset ([uConsole wiki](https://github.com/clockworkpi/uConsole/wiki/How-to-make-phone-call---send-sms-with-4G-extension)). PinePhone-class Linux phones still suffer from "flaky and unpredictable" VoLTE and have no mainstream banking apps ([stateofsurveillance.org](https://stateofsurveillance.org/guides/advanced/open-source-phones/)).

## Screen time falls when mobile internet goes, not when keys arrive

The main point of this report is that none of the recommended keyboard phones limits anything by itself. Each runs the full Play Store. What cuts screen time is taking away the always-available internet. The strongest causal evidence is Castelo et al. in *PNAS Nexus* (2025). In that trial, 467 adults used the Freedom app to **block all mobile internet on their own smartphones for two weeks**, while calls and texts still worked. Attention, mental health and subjective well-being all improved ([PNAS Nexus](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/4/2/pgaf017/8016017)). Among participants who kept the block on for at least 10 of the 14 days, **daily screen time fell from 314 to 161 minutes**, roughly half ([USRTK](https://usrtk.org/healthwire/blocking-mobile-phone-internet-may-boost-mood-mental-health-attention/)). The "at least 10 of 14 days" qualifier is itself a warning: many participants didn't comply. Milder nudges also measurably help. In a six-week PNAS field study of the one sec app, the pause prompt led users to abandon **36% of attempts to open target apps**, and actual opens fell **57%** ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2213114120)).

This shapes how to configure whichever device you choose. The effective part of the "dumbphone" is a phone with **only essential apps installed and no easy path back to feeds**. The carried device should hold the phone app, messaging (Google Messages for RCS, plus WhatsApp or Signal), Google Wallet, your bank apps, an authenticator, Maps and Uber/Lyft, and nothing built for endless consumption. The keyboard phone's 4-inch screen adds passive friction. A physical-token blocker adds active friction by requiring a deliberate physical act to unlock blocked apps. **Brick** costs $59 once with no subscription. It keeps chosen apps locked until you tap the phone to a puck again. It limits you to five emergency unlocks, and a Strict Mode closes the "just delete the app" escape ([Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/reviews/brick-phone-blocker-review/)). **Bloom** costs about $39 and is looser: it's built for scheduled windows and short breaks ([autonomous.ai](https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/brick-vs-bloom), a competitor's blog). One caveat matters for Android keyboard phones. Android blockers depend on Accessibility or Usage Access permissions, which are easier to revoke than iOS Screen Time controls unless the blocker also guards the Settings screens. Choose strict modes and consider having someone else hold the token.

The two-device split finishes the setup. Your existing phone stays home as the media and "long-form" device, and the keyboard phone becomes the only thing you carry. That's the lifestyle Clicks built the Communicator for. It also avoids the payment workarounds that pure dumbphone users face. Those users need a contactless card or a passive NFC ring such as Tapster ([Tapster](https://gotapster.com/)), and they give up app banking and rideshare when away from home. Plan the 2FA move before switching. TOTP codes and passkeys have to be moved deliberately, and whichever device holds your bank app's push approvals should be the one you carry. That last point is the researchers' inference, not a sourced claim. If you currently use an iPhone, note that **none of these Android keyboard phones can run iMessage**. Group chats will move to RCS.

## Your Power Keyboard makes a free trial run possible this week

You already own the one product that lets you rehearse the whole setup without buying anything. The Clicks Power Keyboard is a backlit Bluetooth QWERTY with a 2,150 mAh battery that doubles as a power bank. It **snaps magnetically onto any MagSafe iPhone or Qi2 Android phone** and pairs with up to three devices; hold "123" and press "0" to pair ([Liliputing](https://liliputing.com/clicks-power-keyboard-is-a-magnetic-thumb-keyboard-wireless-power-bank-for-your-phone/); [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/reviews/clicks-power-keyboard-review/)). TechCrunch notes it brings BlackBerry-style typing to any phone "with some compromises" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/10/clicks-power-keyboard-brings-blackberry-style-typing-to-any-phone-with-some-compromises/)). Here's the trial. Put it on your current phone. Install a minimalist launcher. Move social, video and browser apps behind a Brick or Bloom token, or behind one sec if you want zero hardware cost. Then live with it for two weeks, the same length as the Castelo trial. If your screen time roughly halves, you may not need a new phone at all. If you keep unlocking your way back, that's the case for a separate carried device with those apps never installed.

The Power Keyboard adds typing speed but no friction. It's also redundant on the Communicator, the Titan 2 Elite and the Minimal Phone, since each has a built-in keyboard. After you switch, its best use is on the home phone or a tablet. On a phone without magnets, such as most E-ink Android phones, it would need a MagSafe ring. That's the researchers' inference, since its behavior on non-magnetic phones wasn't documented.

## Conclusion

Asking for a "dumbphone" that still handles tap-to-pay and banking really means asking for a **smartphone that behaves like a dumbphone**. Once that's clear, the choice gets simpler. The hardware question comes down to which certified Android keyboard phone to carry. The real screen-time question is how hard it is to get back to the feed. The randomized evidence points to a separate carried device that simply lacks the addictive apps, backed by a lock you can't casually undo. A keyboard makes that life more pleasant but doesn't enforce it.

In practice, the Power Keyboard in your drawer is worth more than a new phone as a first step. It turns the next two weeks into a cheap experiment that tells you whether software friction is enough or whether you need physical separation. The Communicator's October 1 price rise makes you decide before that experiment ends. So the rational move is to reserve only if you already know you want a dedicated keyboard phone, and otherwise to treat the Titan 2 Elite as an always-available fallback at a similar price.
